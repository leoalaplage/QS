How the three figures are computed

FCF is operating cash flow minus capital expenditure, per fiscal year, taken from XBRL filings with the SEC. A window is only used when its fiscal years are consecutive: a one-year gap would distort both the growth rate and the fit without showing it.

CAGR — compound annual growth of FCF from the first to the last year of the window. Undefined when either end is negative: going from −50 to +100 is a turnaround, and no annual rate describes it.

R² — how regular that growth was. A least-squares line is fitted to the logarithm of FCF. Compound growth is an exponential, and the logarithm straightens it, so R² = 1 means “grew at exactly the same rate every year”. Two companies can share a CAGR of 12% while one has an R² of 0.98 and the other 0.35 — same destination, very different ride. Undefined when any year has negative FCF, which has no logarithm.

FCF yield CV — the coefficient of variation (standard deviation over mean) of the FCF yield, itself free cash flow divided by market capitalisation at each year end. This one says nothing about the business and everything about its price: a low CV is a company the market has valued consistently, a high CV one that has swung between enthusiasm and neglect. Sample standard deviation; undefined on a negative mean.

Data source: SEC EDGAR (XBRL) for cash flows, market quotes for the yield.
