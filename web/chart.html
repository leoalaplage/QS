Search companies by name or ticker, stack several metrics on one chart, and read every point by hovering it. Financial metrics come from the XBRL data filed with the SEC; on top of those, QS reads each company’s earnings releases to pull the operating indicators no XBRL tag carries — retention rate, room nights, gross bookings, take rate, remaining performance obligations — and charts them like any other metric.

Key figures

How each metric is computed

Every figure comes from XBRL tags filed with the SEC — nothing is estimated or modelled. Ratios are computed period by period from the two underlying series, never by mixing periods.

What the data covers

Single source: XBRL filings with the SEC. That covers the ~10,400 companies listed in the United States, in both the us-gaap and ifrs-full taxonomies — so foreign companies listed there are included (ASML in EUR, Novo Nordisk in DKK, TSMC in TWD, SAP in EUR). Figures are shown in the currency they were reported in.

Out of reach: a company that is not listed in the United States has no SEC filings at all — Constellation Software (Toronto) or Hermès (Paris) cannot be found here, whatever you search for.

Quarterly and TTM: cash-flow statements are filed cumulatively from the start of the fiscal year (3, 6, 9 then 12 months). Discrete quarters are rebuilt by differencing consecutive cumulative filings, and the four quarters of a year are checked against the reported annual total. Where a quarter is both reported directly and rebuildable, the two are compared and any disagreement is reported.

Comparing a quarter to a year is misleading for seasonal businesses: Apple collects a large share of its cash in the December quarter, so its quarterly FCF margin runs well above its annual one. Use TTM when you want a figure comparable to the annual one.
