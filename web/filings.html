Figures pulled straight from the reports

Payment volumes, processed transactions, revenue by nature — none of it is tagged in XBRL. It lives in tables inside the 10-Q and the earnings release. QS reads those tables, quarter by quarter, and charts them.

Where each number came from

Every point keeps the filing it was read from and the raw table row, so any figure on the chart can be traced back to its line in the report.

How the extraction works, and where it stops

Filings are read as tables, not as text. Flattened to text, a filing table falls apart: labels detach from their figures and “Consumer debit” ends up on one line with its six amounts on the next. Read as rows and cells, the first cell carries the label and the rest carry the values in column order.

Positions are derived, never hard-coded. Visa’s 10-Q used to put a “% Change” column after each region, so the worldwide total sat in the 7th figure; today it sits in the 5th. A fixed rank would silently return a U.S. figure where a global one is expected. The rule says “third of three regions” instead, and holds on both sides of the change.

Each table dates itself. Visa reports volumes one quarter in arrears — the report for the quarter ended March carries volumes for the three months ended December. Periods are read from the table header, so the shift is handled. A table that does not state a period is skipped rather than dated from the filing.

Fiscal fourth quarters are missing from the 10-Q series: that quarter is covered by the 10-K, whose tables are annual. The earnings-release series have no such gap.

Data source: SEC EDGAR — 10-Q reports and 8-K earnings releases.
